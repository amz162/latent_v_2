### 4. Z-Score Mean Reversion

Use a statistically defensible trailing reference series.

Do not assume the raw price level is stationary.

Prefer a documented rolling log-price deviation or suitable stationary residual.

Ensure the rolling mean and standard deviation never include future observations.

### 5. Ornstein-Uhlenbeck

Fit the AR(1)/OU relationship on a trailing history.

Do not clamp a non-mean-reverting fitted coefficient into a stationary range and pretend that it passed the stationarity test.

Verify:

- Valid coefficient range.
- Stability.
- Statistical evidence.
- Residual diagnostics.
- Mean-reversion half-life.
- Adequate observation count.

### 6. Linear Regression Channel

Use a trailing regression window.

Correct index alignment between historical observations and the signal timestamp.

Do not use the current unknown future close when calculating the decision.

### 7. Pairs Trading — Kalman Hedge Ratio

The provided file hardcodes ENGRO/OGDC and assumes a cointegrated pair.

Do not assume this pair is cointegrated.

Verify cointegration using historical training data only.

Use a rolling hedge estimate with proper point-in-time alignment.

Check synchronized source dates and missing bars.

This strategy is **context-only / research-only by default** because the existing beta execution policy is long-only and a conventional hedged pairs position may require a short leg.

Do not include it in an executable bullish/bearish trade vote unless a fully supported, authorized execution structure exists for both legs.

Display the reason for its exclusion.

### 8. Variance Ratio — Lo-MacKinlay

Do not treat the simple rolling variance ratio in the supplied file as a full statistical test.

Use a documented, correctly implemented statistic and the appropriate finite-sample/heteroskedasticity assumptions.

Only use it as a regime or momentum/mean-reversion context gate when sample quality supports it.

Report insufficient history and test validity.

### 9. Hurst Exponent

The supplied Hurst calculation uses price levels.

Review the estimator and use an appropriate return/residual series with sufficient observations.

Add estimator diagnostics and sample-size warnings.

Hurst is a regime descriptor, not an independent bullish or bearish prediction.

### 10. Autocorrelation Filter

Calculate rolling lag-1 autocorrelation on returns using past data only.

Use an adequate observation count and report uncertainty/significance.

Treat it as a context or regime gate rather than an independent trade prediction when statistical support is weak.

### 11. Volatility Breakout — Donchian + ATR

Calculate Donchian thresholds from completed prior bars, using the appropriate shift.

Use a consistent ATR method.

A breakout recognized at session close must use the next eligible session's execution price for hypothetical entry.

Do not execute on the same close used to discover the breakout.

### 12. Bollinger Band Width Regime

Calculate BBW and its derivatives with the existing platform's consistent Bollinger formula.

Treat expansion and contraction as volatility-regime context.

Do not automatically interpret BBW expansion as bullish or contraction as bearish.

Do not describe a strategy as long volatility or short volatility without an actual supported instrument and validated methodology.

- Trend/momentum.

Do not give arbitrary confidence weights because one strategy has a larger signal magnitude.

- Show positive, negative and neutral counts.
- Show the number of independent strategy families represented.
- Show the reasons for mixed or insufficient evidence.
