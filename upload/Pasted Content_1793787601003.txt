# PHASE 6 — VOLATILITY ANALYTICS WINDOW

Create a separate Volatility Analytics screen/window within Latent.pk.

It must be distinct from the Golden Cross window.

The screen must use real verified OHLCV data.

Do not use synthetic prices for market conclusions.

Allow the user to choose symbol, date range and window size.

## A. Close-to-close variance

Calculate daily log return:

\[
r_t=\ln(C_t/C_{t-1})
\]

Calculate rolling sample variance and annualize:

\[
Var_{CC,t}=252\times Var(r_t)
\]

Show:

- Daily variance.
- Annualized variance.
- Annualized volatility.
- Rolling window.
- Valid sample count.

## B. Parkinson variance

Use genuine high and low values.

\[
Var_{P}=
\frac{1}{4\ln(2)}
\operatorname{Mean}\left[\ln(H_t/L_t)^2\right]
\]

Annualize exactly once:

\[
Var_{P,ann}=252\times Var_{P}
\]

Document the estimator's assumptions and limitations, including drift and jump treatment.

## C. Garman-Klass variance

Use:

\[
Var_{GK}=
\operatorname{Mean}\left[
0.5\ln(H_t/L_t)^2
-(2\ln(2)-1)\ln(C_t/O_t)^2
\right]
\]

Annualize exactly once:

\[
Var_{GK,ann}=252\times Var_{GK}
\]

Verify units and report estimator limitations.

Do not silently clip negative estimator values in the underlying research data.

If a negative estimate occurs:

- Record it.
- Report its frequency.
- Diagnose data or estimator assumptions.
- Avoid taking the square root of a negative value.
- Show volatility as unavailable for that observation where appropriate.

## D. Bollinger Band Width

Calculate:

\[
Middle_t=SMA_{20}(C)
\]

\[
Upper_t=Middle_t+2\sigma_{20}
\]

\[
Lower_t=Middle_t-2\sigma_{20}
\]

\[
BBW_t=\frac{Upper_t-Lower_t}{Middle_t}
\]

Use consistent price basis and document the standard-deviation convention.

## E. First and second BBW derivatives

First difference:

\[
BBW_{d1,t}=BBW_t-BBW_{t-1}
\]

Second difference:

\[
BBW_{d2,t}=BBW_{d1,t}-BBW_{d1,t-1}
\]

These are discrete derivatives per valid trading session, not literal continuous-time derivatives.

Account for missing sessions and irregular observations.

Do not calculate a derivative across a missing-data gap as though one regular period passed.

## F. Variance derivatives

For each selected rolling variance estimator, calculate:

- Close-to-close variance first difference.
- Close-to-close variance second difference.
- Parkinson variance first difference.
- Parkinson variance second difference.
- Garman-Klass variance first difference.
- Garman-Klass variance second difference.

Provide the relevant derivatives for both daily and annualized series where useful, without mixing units.

Document:

- First-order change.
- Second-order change.
- Window length.
- Time basis.
- Annualization.
- Missing-value treatment.
