The user receives PSX market data with an approximately 15-minute delay.

The observed schedule is:

| Actual PSX market time | Data appears in the application |
|---|---|
| 09:30 AM — market opens | 09:45 AM |
| 10:00 AM | 10:15 AM |
| 10:15 AM | 10:30 AM |
| 12:00 PM | 12:15 PM |
| 03:15 PM | 03:30 PM |
| 03:30 PM — market closes | 03:45 PM — final delayed update |

This means the application receives delayed market information. The market timestamp and the time the application receives the information are different.

**The 15-minute provider delay must be handled explicitly throughout the ingestion, monitoring, execution, APIs and UI.**

Do not mistake the provider's 15-minute delay for proof that it supplies actual 15-minute OHLC candles.

Verify the API's actual data type before implementation.

# PHASE 6 — HANDLE THE DAILY OHLC SAME-CANDLE PROBLEM

The existing historical database contains daily bars. A daily bar cannot determine whether TP or SL was hit first if both were touched.

Example:

- Entry 100.
- TP 102.
- SL 98.
- Daily high 104.
- Daily low 98.
- Daily close 98.

The daily record cannot prove which level was touched first.

Do not treat the SL-first convention as a verified actual market sequence.

## Intraday-resolution rules

When genuine 15-minute OHLC bars exist:

- If only SL is touched, record a simulated SL trigger.
- If only TP is touched, record a simulated TP event according to strategy rules.
- If neither is touched, continue monitoring.
- If both SL and TP are touched in one 15-minute candle, mark `ambiguous_same_bar`.

A 15-minute candle may still be insufficient to determine the sequence.

Do not claim the candle's open/high/low/close establishes the exact path.

If genuine 1-minute/tick data is not available, ambiguity remains.

## Delayed observation execution semantics

A critical distinction:

A price event may occur at 10:00 and only become visible to the application at 10:15.

Because the shadow system has not placed a real stop order at the exchange:

- Do not claim that an actual stop execution happened at 10:00.
- Record `event_market_timestamp` only if supported by the source data.
- Record `detected_at` when the app received the observation.
- Record the observed price.
- Record the delay.
- For delayed-observation simulation, use the first available observable price and applicable gap/slippage assumptions.
- Do not automatically fill at the nominal stop price when the only actual observation is already beyond it.

If a hypothetical protective stop is modelled as a resting exchange order in a historical simulation, label that as a separate assumed-order backtest, not an observed paper fill.

Maintain two distinct execution modes:

1. `delayed_observation_shadow` — based on actual delayed information available to the application.
2. `intraday_bar_backtest` — hypothetical execution using verified historical intraday bars and documented fill assumptions.

Never merge the results without clearly explaining the difference.

## Snapshot-only limitation

If the provider supplies only delayed price snapshots:

- Check whether the observed price is beyond SL/TP.
- Record the actual observed crossing when supported.
- Do not claim that an unobserved intraperiod high/low was touched.
- A price may cross a stop and recover between snapshots; the app cannot know that from snapshots alone.
- Mark such outcomes `observation_resolution_limited` where appropriate.
